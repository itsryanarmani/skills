# Psychology levers

Pages per lever, out of 54, from the script. A page can use several. Each page's lever list came from a human-style teardown of its screenshot, so treat the counts as "the reviewer saw it", not as measured effect. "Example" names the page element where the lever ranks highest on its page.

| Lever | n | Mechanism | Risk | Honest use | Example element |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Authority | 37 | A credential or endorsement lowers the effort of judging | Borrowed fame; unverifiable titles | State your own checkable record: years, count of guides, a named source | A row of endorsement cards with faces (`brendon-buchard-performance-coach-leads`) |
| Specificity | 35 | A precise promise lets the right reader self-select | Precision you cannot back is a false claim | Name the exact item, format and time to use it | A heading that says how many articles are read each week (`brainpint-curated-newsletter-signup`) |
| Social proof | 33 | Others chose this, so it is safe | Fake or atypical results; walls of cards pad the page | Only real, permitted quotes; else use process facts (see `honest-adaptation.md`) | Many result cards around the offer (`ads-course-ad-system`) |
| Simplicity | 30 | One step, no choice, no friction | None, unless it hides the cost | One field, one button, the same label every time | One email field above one button (`brendon-buchard-productivity-sheet-download`) |
| Identity and aspiration | 29 | "People like me do this" | Flattery that excludes or misleads | Describe who it is for and who it is not for | A tile list of "whether you are..." cases (`amy-porterfield-25k-course-creator-waitlist`) |
| Scarcity | 25 | Limited time or seats raise the cost of waiting | Fake or resetting limits break trust and some laws | Use only a real date, a real seat cap, a real end of a bonus | Dated session buttons (`jenna-kutcher-podcast-launch-class`) |
| Anchoring | 24 | A high reference makes the price look small | A price you never charged is a false-price claim | Compare with a real alternative cost, such as a named paid course or an hour of your time | A list of items each tagged with a value (`brendon-buchard-coach-certification-program`) |
| Loss aversion | 22 | Avoiding a loss motivates more than a gain | Fear copy that exaggerates harm | Name a real cost of the current way: time, a recurring error | A question headline about being the main risk (`alex-harmozi-scale-business-workshop`) |
| Reciprocity | 18 | A free gift creates a sense of return | Gifts with strings, or "free" that is not | Give a real, usable piece first: the checklist | A vault of free templates (`jenna-kutcher-ai-powered-course-academy-waitlist`) |
| Commitment and consistency | 18 | A small yes makes a larger one easier | Pressure; a quiz that only sells | Let the free step teach one thing the paid kit builds on | A "for serious people only" band with commitments (`ali-lokhandwala-amazon-masterclass`) |
| Liking | 17 | We follow people we find warm and similar | Staged intimacy | Write in your real voice; show how you work | An aside that admits the quiz is limited (`jenna-kutcher-business-growth-quiz`) |
| Curiosity gap | 14 | An open question pulls the reader on | Click-bait that the page does not close | Close the gap on the same page | A headline that names a paradox (`codie-sanchez-boardroom-growth`) |

Two more levers appear once each (risk reduction, unity). Do not build on them.

## How to choose

1. Start with simplicity, specificity and reciprocity. They need no outside proof and carry the lowest risk.
2. Add authority only with a fact a visitor can check.
3. Use scarcity and anchoring only when the underlying fact is real. If it is not, leave them out; the page still works (see the lead-magnet blueprint: 0 of 8 use urgency).
4. Re-read each lever with this test: could a visitor check it in two minutes? If not, cut it.
