# Honest adaptation

Case: a small niche guide business with a free checklist and a paid kit. It has no real testimonials, so it makes no income or result claims, no fake scarcity, no timers. This file says what to put in each gap the patterns would fill. It is a method, not a guarantee of results.

## What to drop and what replaces it

| Common move (n of 54) | Drop | Put in its place |
|---|---|---|
| Testimonials (37) | Invented quotes, stock faces, "as seen in" without a feature | The sample itself: show one page of the checklist and one of the kit |
| Result numbers (43) | Any outcome figure you did not measure | Counts of what exists: number of items, steps, sources, pages |
| Authority (37) | Unverifiable titles | Your method, your sources, and a "last updated" date |
| Urgency (25) | Timers, "only 11 left", rising prices that never rise | A real reason to act now, or none. A page with no urgency is normal for opt-ins (0 of 8) |
| Anchoring (22) | A struck-through price you never charged | The real price next to what a mistake costs the reader, stated as a fact |
| Guarantee (13) | A conditional guarantee with traps | A plain refund window you honour, with the terms in one sentence |
| Income claims (27) | All of them | The job the kit does, in plain words |

## Proof you can show without testimonials

- **Checkable facts.** Where the data comes from, who wrote it, the date you last checked each item, and the count of items.
- **Sources.** Link every rule, price or date the guide states to its official page. A dated source list is proof a reader can test.
- **Process specifics.** How you make the guide: what you check, what you reject, how you update it. Concrete steps read as proof because they can be wrong in public.
- **Free sample.** The free checklist is the proof. Make it good, make it findable, and show it on the paid page.
- **Honest limits.** Say who the kit is not for and what it does not cover. This builds trust and filters weak fits (pattern 11).
- **A named person.** Who you are, what you did, one verifiable fact. No story that cannot be checked.
- **Real feedback later.** When a buyer writes to you, ask permission and quote them exactly with their consent. Until then, leave the slot empty. Do not fill it.

## Page plan (two pages)

1. **Checklist page**, lead-magnet blueprint: 1.8 screens, 75 words in the first screen, about 410 words, 3 buttons, one label. Person or product mock-up in the hero. No nav. No urgency. No price.
2. **Kit page**, the long-course order shortened: problem, what is inside, sample page, method and sources, who it is not for, price, refund terms, FAQ. Show the real price once, near the button. Start well below the 10.2-screen course median; you have less to argue.

## Checks before you publish

- Every sentence with a number has a source or is a count of something you can list.
- No word implies a result: "earn", "grow", "guaranteed", "proven".
- No clock, counter or "limited" word unless a real, stated limit exists.
- A stranger could verify each claim in two minutes. If not, cut it.
- A guide that touches law, health or money gets a short, plain scope note and a date.
