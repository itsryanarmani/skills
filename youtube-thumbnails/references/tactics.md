# Tactics

## The 120 px test

The `inference-sh` thumbnail skill says most phone viewers first see a thumbnail about 120 px wide (we did not measure this). Look at the design
at that width. They must still see the feeling (colour and expression), the
subject (composition) and the words (if any). A muddy blur means redesign.

## Expressions

Both source skills (see `inspiration.md`) say faces beat faceless thumbnails; we did not test it. The expression sets the feeling.

| Expression | Strength | Pair with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Surprise, shock | Highest | A reveal, a discovery, "wait, what" |
| Curiosity (head tilt, hand on chin, brow up) | High | How-to, a question, "why" |
| Excitement | High | An announcement, a result |
| Worry, sad | Medium-high | A problem, a warning, "another X?" |
| Confidence (arms crossed, calm smile) | Medium | Authority, news |
| Neutral | Lowest | Only for a deliberately plain brand |

Eyes at the camera make a connection. Eyes toward the words or an object steer
attention. Put the face on one side and the words on the other.

## Colour

Use at most three colours. Contrast the background with the words and the
person. Saturate a little more than real life, because thumbnails sit beside
bright app chrome. Do not put same-temperature colours side by side.

| Pair | Mood |
| --- | --- |
| Yellow and black | Urgency, tech, lists |
| Red and white | Energy |
| Blue and orange | Professional contrast |
| Green and white | Growth |
| Purple and yellow | Creative, premium |
| White and dark | Clean, minimal |

## Text

- Four words or fewer. Six is the ceiling.
- Bold sans-serif, large, with strong contrast. Thin fonts vanish at 120 px.
- Do not repeat the title. Add a feeling, a question or a tension.
- Good: a question ("Why X?"), a statement ("Not another resume."), a name.
- Bad: a sentence, small labels, stacked captions, punctuation clutter.
- Give every line of one block the same font size.

## Composition

- Rule of thirds. One focal point. Space on one side for words.
- Keep the bottom-right (duration badge) and bottom-left (chapter marker) clear.
- Keep an edge margin. Crops differ by device.
- Cut out the person from a busy background, or use a flat colour behind them.

## Pairing with the title

The title says what, the thumbnail says how it feels. Both say the same single
idea. If the title already has the number, the thumbnail does not repeat it.

## Test one thing at a time

Face against no face. Surprise against curiosity. Warm against cool. Text against
no text. Bright against dark. Left-facing against right-facing. Change one
variable per test and keep the rest.

## Tactics for many videos at once

- Fix the layout and the pose set, change only the item text. The channel then
  looks like one brand.
- Use the same background colour for one batch, or one colour per topic.
- Read the longest name in the batch first. A layout that fits it fits all.
- For personal or private videos, a plain candid or a "for <name>" tag feels
  more honest than a loud claim.
